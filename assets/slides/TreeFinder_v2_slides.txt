   Beyond Forest Clearance
   Individual tree mortality are missed due to low resolutions.
   A recent study found that 60% of dead trees were scattered in California
   Missing tree mortality signals can delay risk detection and increase downstream costs in maintenance, restoration, and public safety.
   Cheng et al. Nature Communication, 2024

===== slide 7 (18 paragraphs; media: image41.emf, image38.png, image31.emf, image40.png, hdphoto7.wdp, image39.png)
   Related Work
   Limitations of existing datasets for individual tree mortality detections
   Drone-based datasets provide very high resolution (e.g., deadtree.earth), but are hard to scale
   No comprehensive benchmarking for dead tree segmentation, especially under challenging visual conditions
   Comparison to Existing Datasets
   Drone images from deadtree.earth
   NAIP images from our TreeFinder

===== slide 9 (7 paragraphs; media: image42.png, image43.png)
   Dataset Construction
   Collected 1,000 sites distributed across forest areas
   Applied multispectral representations to better annotate dead trees
   Incorporated multi-temporal information to validate annotations
   Achieved 95% agreement in the cross-annotator assessment

   Conclusion
   TreeFinder
   A high-resolution, US-scale benchmark dataset
   1,000 sites over 48 states with 23,000-hectare coverage
   Comprehensive scenarios for generalization test
   17 evaluation scenarios
   Diverse sets of ML models for benchmark
