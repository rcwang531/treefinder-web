Why scattered tree death matters

Forests are among the planet's largest carbon sinks, yet trees that die one by one rarely show up in national forest monitoring.

  • ~30%

    Carbon

    Forests absorb about 30% of human CO₂ emissions each year. Dead trees left uncounted bias estimates of that uptake.

    Bellassen & Luyssaert, Nature 2014
  • 60%

    Hidden mortality

    Of the 91.4 million dead trees mapped in California in 2020, 60% stood alone or in small groups, which state-level monitoring largely misses.

    Cheng et al., Nature Communications 2024

  • Wildfire & forest health

    Standing dead trees become fuel for future wildfires, and scattered deaths can mark drought stress, pests and disease spreading through a stand.

    Cheng et al., Nature Communications 2024

  • Costs & safety

    Missing these signals delays risk detection and raises downstream costs for forest maintenance, restoration and public safety.

Beyond forest clearance

Large-scale forest loss, from tropical deforestation to clear-cuts and wildfires, is now mapped and studied worldwide3456. A single dead crown is different: it is a few meters across, smaller than one pixel of the 10–30 m satellites behind most national monitoring.

Why NAIP?

  • Wall-to-wall coverage of the contiguous US, not just sites a drone can reach
  • Free and public imagery from the USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program
  • Consistent bands: red, green, blue and near-infrared at 0.6 m or finer
  • Regular collection: each state is re-flown every 2–3 years

The TreeFinder dataset

Careful annotation

  • Pixel-level dead-tree masks drawn on R-G-B, NIR-R-G and NDVI views
  • Labels validated against multi-temporal NAIP imagery
  • 3 rounds of refinement and 95%+ cross-annotator agreement

A benchmark for generalization

Six segmentation models in three families (U-Net and DeepLabV3+; ViT, SegFormer and Mask2Former; and the remote-sensing foundation model DOFA) are evaluated in 17 scenarios at two difficulty levels: random splits plus cross-region, cross-climate and cross-forest-type shifts.

Mask2Former scores best on F1, precision and IoU. Broader spatial coverage in training matters most for generalization, and models are more sensitive to climate-zone shifts than to forest-type shifts.

Help us find more dead trees

TreeFinder grows with its community. Approved contributors can:

  • Validate labels

    Click a labeled polygon on the map and answer one question: is this tree dead?

  • Draw new labels

    Outline dead trees at any of the 1,000 sites on the latest NAIP imagery, then download your polygons as GeoJSON and upload them to the shared collection.
